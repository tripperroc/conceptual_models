# Conceptual Models for Teaching and Learning Computer Science Theory

This repository contains a collection of lessons for teaching and learning computer science theory using *conceptual models*. These lessons are used in *Gems of Computer Science Theory* courses taught at The University of Rochester and Rochester Institute of Technology, by computer science professors Lane Hemaspaandra and Christopher Homan, respectively, and developed by them in collaboration with Kimberly Fluet, PhD of the Warner School of Education. We offer them here for public use, subject to the terms of https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0/.

## What are conceptual models?

Imagine being able to see inside the minds of your students as you teach? How would that affect how you explain your content. What if, additionally, you could tailor the material individually to each student, based on what you see in their minds?

*Conceptual models* have the power to do that. Rather than submit fully written proofs, students submit figures, code, and illustrations explaining core concepts. These allow students to express problems using words, ideas, or images that are understandable to them.

## Organization

The "lessons" folder contains all weekly lessons developed by Christopher M. Homan for his "Gems" course.
